ROBO Global maps the humanoid parts stack

Image © Humanoid Guide — A new guide from the ROBO Global Robotics and Automation Index sorts the humanoid bill of materials into three systems — a sensor-and-compute brain, a body of rotary and linear actuators with precision gearing, and an energy layer where battery weight remains the core constraint. Stabilus and Synapticon pair up to mass-produce humanoid joints

Motion-control manufacturer Stabilus and drive-tech specialist Synapticon have formed a strategic partnership to jointly develop and build integrated actuators for humanoid robots, covering joints from the head and wrists down to the hips and knees. Small-series production starts at Stabilus plants in Europe in 2027, with large-scale output planned in North America from 2028 to sit close to major humanoid makers, according to the companies.

Sensors

RoboSense goes fully solid-state with its E2 robot lidar

The Shenzhen lidar maker unveiled E2, a fully solid-state digital lidar built on its in-house SPAD-SoC that fits up to a 180-degree field of view behind just two exposed optical windows, aimed at humanoids, quadrupeds, drones and mowers, according to RoboSense. Two companion chipsets — Phoenix for long-range precision and Peacock for image-grade wide-angle 3D perception — debut alongside it, with the company counting AgiBot, Pudu Robotics and Geek+ among robotics partners already on its lidars.

Sensors

A robot fingertip that feels by changing color

Researchers at Queen Mary University of London and Italian partner universities built a fingertip whose synthetic skin shifts color as it deforms, letting an embedded camera map contact pressure and surface topology at 100-micrometer resolution with zero computational latency, according to the team. The mechanochromic design could give robot hands quantitative touch without packing electronics into the fingertip, though durability in real-world use is still unproven.

Tacta shows its robot hand — and the glove that trains it

Image © Tacta Systems — Palo Alto's Tacta Systems unveiled TactaBot, pairing a 15-joint Tacta Hand driven by fluidic tendon actuation with a sensor-laden skill-capture glove and an AI engine trained on recorded human technique, according to the company. US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer calls Beijing's compliance "not perfect."
