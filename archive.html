Analysis

Why humanoid hardware, not AI, sets the pace

Motors, power electronics and sensing — not smarter models — determine what a humanoid can actually do, argues Allegro MicroSystems' Andreas Friedrich in The Robot Report: a humanoid juggles 30-plus degrees of freedom against six or seven for a typical industrial robot, and every gram of electronics adds to the energy needed to move it. He makes the case for 48-volt architectures, which he says cut current draw by a factor of four and wiring losses by a factor of 16 versus 12 volts, and for designing sensing, motor control, power and thermal management as one integrated system.
